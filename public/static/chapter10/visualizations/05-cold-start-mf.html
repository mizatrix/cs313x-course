Part 1 · The Cold-Start Problem

Una just signed up (empty row); Item X was just added (empty column). With no overlapping ratings, similarity to other users/items is undefined — pure collaborative filtering cannot recommend.

Why CF Fails Here

Neighborhood CF computes similarity from co-rated entries. A new user shares zero ratings with anyone, so every cosine/Pearson similarity is 0/0 — undefined. The same holds for a new item's column. Fallbacks inject an external signal until enough ratings accumulate.

Part 2 · Matrix Factorization — R ≈ U·Vᵀ
R̂(u, i) = pᵤ · qᵢ = Σf pu,f · qi,f

Instead of comparing whole rows, MF learns a short latent-factor vector for every user (pᵤ) and every item (qᵢ). A predicted rating is just their dot product. Drag the 2 factors to reconstruct the observed rating.

Key Insight

MF turns a huge, sparse R into two thin tables U (n×k) and V (m×k) with k ≪ n, m. The latent factors are learned, not labeled — "action" / "romance" are just our interpretation. Predicting an unobserved cell is one dot product away.
